## Nothing is encoded

Every other showcase in this section argues about a mapping: which measurement drives which part
of the glyph, and whether the reader can decode it. Here there is no mapping to argue about. The
item *is* a picture, and the only question left is the one this library exists for — is there
room to draw it.

That makes the image case the cleanest statement of the problem. A thumbnail small enough not to
overlap is a thumbnail too small to recognise, and shrinking until the overlaps go away destroys
exactly the thing you came to look at. Switch **Layout** and the same 560 photographs become
individually visible, at a size set by the grid rather than by the closest pair.

The mount around each thumbnail is the species. It is there to answer a question the pictures
cannot: whether the projection grouped what a botanist would group.

## Where the projection is honest, and where it is not

Sunflowers and bluebells separate cleanly — strong, unmistakable colour. The white flowers do
not: snowdrops and lily of the valley sit in the same region, because a projection computed from
image content is largely a projection of colour and shape, and both are small white flowers on
green.

That is worth seeing rather than being told, and you can only see it once the images are big
enough to compare side by side. On the raw projection that neighbourhood is a smear.
